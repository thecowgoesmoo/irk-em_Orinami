Orinami

The input signal is a position on a strip of screen. Bend the strip, spin it about its hinge, and read where the lit point lands on two perpendicular axes. Those two readings are the outputs.

Reading the figure

Lit point
The input sample, as a position along the strip from one end (−1) to the other (+1).
Default out
The point's shadow on the horizontal axis, traced downward over time.
Orthogonal out
Its shadow on the vertical axis, traced leftward over time.
Dashed outline
Where the springs are trying to hold the strip: the Separation and Rotation settings.

What moves it

Separation
Rest length of the spring joining the two end masses. 1 is flat, 0 brings the ends together, below 0 winds the strip up to four turns.
Rotation
Rest angle of the torsion spring at the hinge.
Shape
How the bend is distributed: sharp pleats, an even arc, or square corners.
Mass
The end masses. They also set how hard the strip is to spin, so curling it in speeds the spin up.
Damping
Below zero the spring feeds energy in and that axis oscillates by itself.
Coupling
How strongly the spin throws the end masses outward.

The model

T = ½·M·u′² + ½·I(u)·θ′² M = m/2, I(u) = 2m·(r(u)² + r₀²) V = ½·k_fold·(u − u₀)² + ½·k_rot·(θ − θ₀)² default = y·cos θ − z·sin θ orthogonal = y·sin θ + z·cos θ

u is the separation, θ the rotation, r(u) the distance of the end masses from the hinge, and (y, z) the lit point's position on the bent strip. This page runs the same equations as the module, slowed down so the motion is visible.
